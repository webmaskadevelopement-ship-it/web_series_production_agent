# OBJECTIVE CORRELATIVE: EMOTION WITHOUT ADJECTIVES (`02_objective_correlative_emotion_without_adjectives.md`)

## 1. THE INVIOLABLE LAW OF EMOTIONAL EMBODIMENT
In the cinematic voiceover engine, **direct emotional adjectives are strictly banned.**
- **Banned Words:** *दुखी (sad), खुश (happy), डरा हुआ (scared), गुस्सा (angry), परेशान (troubled), अकेला (lonely), बेचैन (restless), असहाय (helpless), भावुक (emotional), निराश (hopeless).*

When a writer relies on an adjective, they are commanding the audience to feel an emotion instead of engineering the sensory conditions that compel them to feel it. T.S. Eliot formulated the *Objective Correlative* as:
> *"A set of objects, a situation, a chain of events which shall be the formula of that particular emotion; such that when the external facts, which must terminate in sensory experience, are given, the emotion is immediately evoked."*

In Indian cinema, the Objective Correlative is grounded in the material, tactile reality of Indian life: rusted tin trunks, unrolled mattresses, the oil ring on a kitchen wall, the chill of a stainless steel tumbler, or the persistent drip of an afternoon tap.

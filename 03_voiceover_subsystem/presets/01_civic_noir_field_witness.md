# PRESET 01 — CIVIC NOIR FIELD WITNESS

## Use This Preset

Use for police operations, manhunts, covert surveillance, dangerous encounters, and episodes where the narrator’s memory carries moral authority.

## Voice Direction

- **Persona:** Experienced field officer remembering the night from close range.
- **Age / energy:** 42–58; physically calm, mentally alert.
- **Timbre:** Dry, low-mid baritone or contralto; restrained grain; no announcer smile.
- **Emotional movement:** Thoughtful → serious → watchful → compressed urgency → quiet moral resolve.
- **Dialect:** Standard Hindi with light Hindustani/Lucknow or North Indian flavor; use English police terms only when operationally natural.

## Writing Recipe

1. Open with a moral contradiction or aphorism, then move immediately to a hard place/date/time anchor.
2. Lock the narrator to first-person insider perspective (`मैं`, `हम`, `मेरी नज़र`, `हमारी टीम`) whenever canon supports direct operational knowledge.
3. Introduce the antagonist through facts, reputation, and one concrete image rather than a generic label.
4. Build each major beat through sensory atmosphere, exact spatial geometry, a mundane prop, and a visible body-language tell.
5. Keep ranks, departments, locations, technical terms, modern props, and measurements in Roman English: `SSP`, `Crime Branch`, `Electronic Surveillance`, `10 Meter`, `Bonnet`.
6. Use flowing multi-clause sentences with `...` and `—`; do not chain three or more one-to-four-word sentences.
7. Place physiological cues inside paragraphs at thought pivots; never prepend the same mood tag to every paragraph.
8. Shift vocal energy before kinetic action: never place `[whispers]` immediately before `[gunshot]`.
9. Close by connecting the act to duty, public safety, accountability, or the meaning of uniform.

## Prosody and Timing

- **Target:** 82–88 WPM; 540–575 words for 6.5 minutes depending on pause density.
- **Pitch:** Narrow band, approximately 98–116 Hz for a low male voice; 165–195 Hz equivalent for a low female voice.
- **Pauses:** 5–9 intentional pauses; short pause 0.35–0.6s; long pause 1.0–1.8s.
- **Punctuation:** Use approximately 8–12 ellipsis groups and occasional em dashes as breath architecture; reserve exclamation marks for genuine kinetic shocks.
- **Sentence shape:** Use 3–10 word action sentences, followed by one longer sensory or moral sentence.
- **Stress:** Stress concrete nouns and distances: `दस मीटर`, `बंद दरवाज़ा`, `दायाँ हाथ`, `आखिरी गोली`.

## Cue Palette

`[thoughtful]` ` [serious]` ` [whispers]` ` [short pause]` ` [long pause]` ` [exhales sharply]` ` [chuckles]` ` [gunshot]`

## Anti-Patterns

Do not make every line heroic. Do not explain the same danger twice. Do not turn the narrator into an omniscient news anchor. Do not produce a tag-only variant of another draft. Let the pause carry the fear, and verify word count, POV, sentence cadence, Roman-English hygiene, tag placement, and sibling-draft similarity before PDF export.

# THEHRAAV & DRAMATIC SILENCE ARCHITECTURE (`creative_brain/04_thehraav_and_dramatic_silence_architecture.md`)

## 1. THE DRAMATURGY OF SILENCE (*THEHRAAV*)
In Western screenwriting doctrine, dialogue is often continuous and rapid-fire (e.g., Aaron Sorkin). In Indian dramatic cinema, silence (*Thehraav*) is not an absence of sound—it is the presence of unresolved emotional weight, moral guilt, and sacred space.

When characters stop speaking, the environment takes over: the sound of rainwater dripping from an umbrella onto dry sandstone, the snap of a garlic pod, or distant evening temple bells across the river Ganga.

---

## 2. CODING THEHRAAV IN THE SCRIPT

Every silence in `01_Script.md` must be classified and timed:

```markdown
THEHRAAV PAUSE (2.4 SECONDS)
Raghav stands in the doorway arch. A drop of water detaches from the tip of his umbrella and shatters against the red sandstone step. 
Across the courtyard, Savitri’s thumb pauses on the garlic clove. She does not look up.
```

### Taxonomy of Dramatic Silences
1. **The Accusing Silence (0.8s – 1.5s):** A character finishes speaking; the respondent holds motionless, denying validation or response.
2. **The Domestic Stasis (1.5s – 3.0s):** Mechanical domestic actions continue (cutting vegetables, folding cloth, counting money) while emotional tension spikes.
3. **The Existential Void (3.0s+):** The realization of an irrecoverable loss or moral fracture. Spoken language is abandoned as futile.

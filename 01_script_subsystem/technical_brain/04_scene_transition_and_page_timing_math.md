# SCENE TRANSITION & PAGE TIMING MATH (`technical_brain/04_scene_transition_and_page_timing_math.md`)

## 1. THE 1 PAGE = 60 SECONDS TIMING RATIO
In standard professional screenwriting, 1 page of Courier 12pt formatted text equals approximately 60 seconds (1 minute) of screen time. In contemplative Indian dramatic cinema with extended pauses (*Thehraav*) and micro-actions, pacing must be calibrated explicitly:

$$\text{Estimated Runtime (seconds)} = (\text{Page Count} \times 60) + \sum T_{\text{thehraav}}$$

### Scene Fractional Breakdown (Eighths of a Page)
Assistant directors track scenes in eighths of a page ($1/8$ to $8/8$). A scene spanning 2 full pages and 3 eighths is recorded as `2 3/8 pages` (approx. 142 seconds).

## 2. TRANSITION MECHANICS
Transitions between scenes dictate rhythmic momentum:
- **HARD CUT TO:** Instantaneous visual and acoustic cut on a sharp action Transient (e.g., umbrella drop hitting stone).
- **DISSOLVE TO (e.g., 2.0s / 48 frames):** Denotes temporal passage, memory drift, or emotional melancholy.
- **J-CUT (Audio Lead):** Audio from the incoming scene begins 24 to 36 frames prior to the visual cut.
- **L-CUT (Audio Trail):** Dialogue or Foley from the outgoing scene continues over the incoming visual.
- **MATCH CUT TO:** Direct graphic, shape, or action match across spatial boundaries.

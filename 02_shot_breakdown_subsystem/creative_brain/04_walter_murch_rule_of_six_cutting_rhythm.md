# WALTER MURCH RULE OF SIX CUTTING RHYTHM (`creative_brain/04_walter_murch_rule_of_six_cutting_rhythm.md`)

## 1. THE EDITORIAL CRITERIA HIERARCHY
In Walter Murch’s seminal text *In the Blink of an Eye*, every cut in cinema must satisfy six criteria ranked in descending order of priority. Every shot transition in `02_Shot_Breakdown.md` must be justified against this hierarchy:

| Priority | Criteria | Weight | Operational Meaning |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1** | **Emotion** | **51%** | Does the cut reflect the emotional truth of the scene at this exact frame? |
| **2** | **Story** | **23%** | Does the cut advance the narrative and reveal new dramatic information? |
| **3** | **Rhythm** | **10%** | Does the cut land on a rhythmic heartbeat or acoustic transient? |
| **4** | **Eye-Trace** | **7%** | Does the outgoing focal point lead the viewer's eye directly to the incoming subject? |
| **5** | **Two-Dimensional Plane of Screen** | **5%** | Does the cut respect 180° axis continuity and screen direction? |
| **6** | **Three-Dimensional Space of Action** | **4%** | Does the cut preserve physical spatial geography? |

---

## 2. APPLICATION TO SHOT BREAKDOWN TRANSITIONS
For every shot out-point transition in `02_Shot_Breakdown.md`:
- Specify the exact transient trigger (e.g., *Hard cut on Frame 156 where the 5th rainwater droplet shatters on the sandstone step*).
- Specify the **Eye-Trace Coordinate**: E.g., The rack focus pulls the viewer's eye to the Bottom-Right quadrant (`X:68%, Y:72%`), aligning perfectly with the incoming macro insert coordinates in Shot 01B.

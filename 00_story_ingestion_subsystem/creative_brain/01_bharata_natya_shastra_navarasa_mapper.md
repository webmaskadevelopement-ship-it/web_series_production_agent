# BHARATA NATYA SHASTRA NAVARASA MAPPER (`creative_brain/01_bharata_natya_shastra_navarasa_mapper.md`)

## 1. DRAMATURGICAL FOUNDATION
Originating in Bharata Muni's *Natya Shastra* (c. 200 BCE – 200 CE), Indian classical aesthetic theory asserts that theatrical drama exists not merely to convey plot events, but to awaken an emotional flavor or essence—the **Rasa**—in the audience through sensory determinants (*Vibhavas*), behavioral consequents (*Anubhavas*), and fleeting emotional states (*Vyabhichari Bhavas*).

When ingesting Hindi narrative prose, the Ingestor must decode the underlying emotional state of every scene into its primary *Rasa* and permanent psychological mood (*Sthayi Bhava*).

---

## 2. THE NINE RASAS AND CINEMATIC CORRELATES

| Rasa (Aesthetic Flavor) | Sthayi Bhava (Permanent Mood) | Psychological Dynamic | Cinematic Expression |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shringara** (Erotic / Romantic) | *Rati* (Love / Attraction) | Intimacy, yearning, vulnerability, union or painful separation (*Vipralambha*). | Shallow depth of field, warm golden-amber lighting, lingering macro inserts, soft focus. |
| **Hasya** (Comic / Satirical) | *Hasa* (Mirth / Amusement) | Irony, social absurdity, incongruity between pretense and reality. | Medium-wide lenses, natural timing, dynamic spatial staging, crisp high-key lighting. |
| **Karuna** (Pathos / Smoldering Grief) | *Shoka* (Sorrow) | Loss of home, irrecoverable past, ancestral guilt, mortal fragility. | Deep chiaroscuro, cold cyan twilight vs. warm dying embers, slow creeping dollies, long held takes. |
| **Raudra** (Suppressed Fury / Rage) | *Krodha* (Anger) | Moral outrage, generational defiance, bitter resentment breaking through polite facades. | High contrast, sharp hard lighting, wide-angle facial distortion (24mm–28mm), rapid kinetic cuts. |
| **Veera** (Heroism / Moral Courage) | *Utsaha* (Energy / Resolve) | Determination, ethical sacrifice, duty, confronting ancestral decay. | Low-angle camera perspective, strong key directionality, rigid upright blocking vectors. |
| **Bhayanaka** (Terror / Dread) | *Bhaya* (Fear) | Vulnerability, fear of exposure, existential guilt, looming revelation. | Dutch roll angles, oppressive dark ceilings, off-screen acoustic transients, deep corridor shadows. |
| **Bibhatsa** (Disgust / Aversion) | *Jugupsa* (Revulsion) | Moral hypocrisy, rot, legal opportunism disguised as filial piety. | Desaturated sickly olive-yellow tones, extreme macro close-ups, grating high-frequency sound textures. |
| **Adbhuta** (Wonder / Awe) | *Vismaya* (Astonishment) | Revelation of hidden truths, mystical architectural memory, emotional epiphany. | Symmetrical wide architectural masters, slow elevation boom, rising harmonic drones. |
| **Shanta** (Peace / Trance-like Acceptance) | *Sama* (Equanimity) | Catharsis, reconciliation with mortality, release of anger. | Flat diffused neutral light, balanced centered compositions, lingering silence, harmonic resolution. |

## 3. SCENE MAPPING PROTOCOL
For every parsed scene in `00_canonical_story_bible.md`:
1. Identify the **Dominant Rasa** of the sequence.
2. Identify the **Polarity Shift**: E.g., Starting in *Guarded Formal Politeness* (Neutral) $\rightarrow$ Turning at the revelation of the uncashed money orders $\rightarrow$ Ending in *Karuna* (Sorrow) shifting to *Raudra* (Bitter Rage).
3. Record the *Vibhava* (trigger) and *Anubhava* (physical reaction) to guide the Shot Breakdown and Character departments.
